"A Dog Called Money" Book 1 systematically introduces the entire process from establishing financial concepts, increasing income, to investment allocation, through a story about financial management. The following are the core financial skills and concepts from the book:

I. Financial Goals and Mindset Building

  1. Clarify Wealth Aspirations:
    • Write down 10 reasons why you want to be rich, and select 3 most important wishes from them as core goals.
    • Goals need to be visualized/concrete:
      • Create a "dream album", collect photos related to your wishes, look at them several times a day and "visualize" them, imagining what it will be like when they are realized.
      • Establish a "dream savings jar" to see actual progress.
  2. Build Confidence (Success Journal):
    • Confidence is key to getting rich. Record at least 5 small things you succeeded in doing each day to build confidence.
    • When encountering difficulties or fears, review your success journal and focus your energy on what you know, what you can do, and what you have.
    • "Do" means "do", avoid words like "try to", "give it a try", "attempt".
    • Don't be influenced by opinions around you; distinguish between "opinion" and "advice".

II. Wealth Creation Principles and Actionability

  1. Solving Problems is Wealth: Solving a problem or providing value for others allows you to earn money.
  2. Action Guidelines:
    • 72-hour rule: When you decide to do something, you must take action within 72 hours, otherwise you might never do it.
    • 10-minute rule: Regardless of the circumstances, insist on spending 10 minutes each day doing things that are significant for the future (e.g., recording a success journal, envisioning the future). Whether in favorable or adverse circumstances, always keep moving forward every day.
  3. Diversified Income: Don't rely solely on a single job; use your interests and strengths to exchange value and find alternative income solutions.

III. Income Allocation and Debt Management

  1. 50-40-10 Income Allocation Method (after deducting basic living expenses):
    • 50% for "nurturing the goose" (savings and financial management, generating dividends).
    • 40% deposited into the "dream savings jar" (accumulating funds for medium-to-short-term goals).
    • 10% for consumption (daily expenses and pocket money).
  2. Four Pieces of Advice for Debt Management:
    • Destroy credit cards: Avoid spending beyond your means.
    • Repay in installments at the minimum standard: Keep more cash for emergencies; don't use all spare money to pay off debt.
    • "Half for debt, half for savings": After deducting living expenses, save half of the remaining funds to nurture the goose, and use the other half for debt repayment, ensuring that you are accumulating wealth while paying off debt.
    • Pre-consumption inquiry: Before consuming, ask yourself, "Is this really necessary?"

IV. Investment Appreciation (Nurturing the "Goose That Lays Golden Eggs")

  1. The Logic of the Goose and Golden Eggs:
    • "Goose" represents the principal, "golden eggs" represent interest or returns. Don't "kill the goose" for immediate consumption; let the principal appreciate continuously through compound interest.
    • When interest income (golden eggs) can support living expenses, financial freedom is achieved.
  2. Principles of Fund Investment:
    • Choose equity funds that are at least 10 years old and large multinational.
    • Long-term holding: Be prepared for a 5-10 year investment cycle, and do not sell due to short-term market downturns.
  3. Financial Calculation Tools:
    • Rule of 72: Use "72 divided by the annualized interest rate" to get the time required for the principal to double; you can also use "72 divided by the inflation rate" to get the time for money to halve in value.

V. Summary of Core Golden Sayings

  • Money is neutral, it neither makes people happy nor brings misfortune; it is merely a magnifying glass that reveals a person's true nature.
  • To live a happier life, one must change oneself, rather than merely expecting the environment to change.
  • Luck is the result of thorough preparation combined with hard work.
